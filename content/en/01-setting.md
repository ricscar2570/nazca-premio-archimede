# PART I — THE GAME

## 1.1  Historical background

The Nazca lines (200 BC – 600 AD) are monumental geoglyphs etched into the coastal desert of southern Peru. The Nazca people created them by removing dark pebbles to reveal the lighter ground beneath. Thousands of straight lines, geometric shapes and colossal animal figures — a 96-metre hummingbird, a 46-metre spider, a 130-metre condor — stretch for hundreds of kilometres across the pampa.

Why? The mystery resisted explanation for centuries. The most convincing answer — the hydrological hypothesis proposed by David Johnson and other researchers — links the lines to underground water routes. The Nazca also built the puquios, subterranean aqueducts that are still functioning today and once irrigated 3,000 hectares of arid land. The lines may have been maps of these hidden flows: tracing the line meant finding the water.

The felling of the huarango — a sacred tree and the ecological foundation of the desert — to expand farmland left the civilisation vulnerable. Around 750 AD, a catastrophic El Niño event destroyed the system. The lines remained. Empty, silent, inexplicable.

## 1.2  The hydrological hypothesis — the game's thematic core

NAZCA adopts the hydrological hypothesis as its narrative and mechanical premise. In the game, the lines are maps of the subsoil. Each clan holds a secret geoglyph card indicating the path to trace — and that path coincides with the pampa's hidden water flows. Searching for water and drawing the geoglyph are the same action.

> [!NOTE]
> The geoglyph is not built. It is discovered. Players remove the darkness of the subsoil by following the water, and what remains — their coloured lines on the board — is a drawing that was always there, hidden in the geology.

## 1.3  Pitch — three sentences

**The game:** You are a Nazca clan in the Peruvian desert. You trace sacred lines across the pampa to find underground water, build puquios to channel it downhill, and irrigate the fields along the river valleys.

**The mechanic:** You plant coloured pegs on the board (pampa) and stretch cords between them: inward to draw the geoglyph, toward the edge to build the canal. The fields sit off the board, in the valley.

**The ending:** At the end, everyone stands up and looks down at the board from above. The cords of the four clans have drawn Spider, Orca, Hummingbird and Condor — the geoglyphs of the pampa. Players vote for the most beautiful.
