# PART XI — END OF GAME AND THE REVELATION

## 11.1  End-game triggers

The game ends in one of three cases, checked at the end of each round:

- A player has exhausted their cord reel (can no longer trace or branch)
- 6 fixed rounds have been completed (maximum timer)
- All players have passed consecutively in the same round without taking any action

The most common trigger is completing 6 rounds.

## 11.2  The Revelation sequence

The Revelation moment is the emotional climax of the game. Follow this sequence carefully and without rushing:

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | All players simultaneously reveal their geoglyph cards — discover which animal each clan was tracing |
| 2 | EVERYONE STANDS UP and steps back at least one metre from the table. Look at the board from above. |
| 3 | A moment of silence. The cords of the four clans have drawn the interwoven geoglyphs of the pampa. |
| 4 | BLIND VOTE — Art Bonus: everyone takes a slip and pencil from the box. In silence, without communicating, each player writes the name of the geoglyph they find most recognisable. All slips are handed in together and revealed simultaneously. Most votes: +2 pt. ANTI-LEADER RULE: if the most-voted geoglyph belongs to the player leading in harvest points, the Art Bonus goes to the second-most-voted instead. Tied votes: higher completion percentage wins. |
| 5 | Reveal the El Niño card: compare total fellings against the threshold. If ≥ threshold: −3 pt to all. |
| 6 | Calculate final scores (see Part XII). |

## 11.3  Why the Revelation works

The moment when everyone stands up is unique in the board game landscape. It replicates the physical act of looking at the real geoglyphs, which are not visible from ground level — only from above. Throughout the game no one has seen the board from above; players were focused on their own pegs and cords. Standing up is a ritual gesture that transforms the play space into something else.

The geoglyph is not created by the players. It is discovered. The Nazca did not build the lines — they revealed them, removing dark pebbles to find the lighter ground beneath. Players do the same: by seeking water along their geoglyph path, they remove the unknown of the subsoil and reveal a drawing that was already hidden in the pampa's geology.
