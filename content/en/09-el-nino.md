# PART IX — EL NIÑO

## 9.1  The environmental threat

El Niño is the collective risk mechanism. The felling of sacred huarango trees left the Nazca civilisation vulnerable. In the game: every felled tree brings the threshold closer. No one knows how much remains.

## 9.2  The global counter

Every felling (by any player) increments the global counter visible to all. The face-down El Niño card has a secret threshold of 4, 5 or 6. It is revealed at the end of the game.

> [!WARNING]
> THE TRIPLE DILEMMA: (1) When to use my free felling? Early (more water + immediate field, but high El Niño risk for all) or late (less risk, but water and field arrive at the wrong moment)? (2) Is the −1 pt worth the +2 water + free field? (3) If I fell, I push El Niño closer for everyone. If I hold back hoping no one else does, but they all do… El Niño triggers anyway and I gain nothing.

## 9.3  Penalties and bonuses

:::twocol
| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Individual penalty | −1 pt per huarango felled (from the player who felled it) |
| El Niño collective penalty | −3 pt to all if total fellings ≥ card threshold |
| Survival bonus | +3 pt to all if no huarango was felled (all 6 trees still standing at game end) |
| Cumulation | Penalties stack: a player who felled 2 trees and triggers El Niño loses −2 pt individual + −3 pt El Niño = −5 pt total |
:::

El Niño does NOT end the game early and does NOT reset harvests. It is a final score penalty.

## 9.4  Free action 1×/game

Each player has only 1 free felling action per game (not per round). It is taken outside the 3-action count, in any round.

The choice of round is a genuine strategic decision: the water and free irrigated field are worth more in rounds 2–3 (allowing immediate sowing) than in round 6 (too late to mature).
